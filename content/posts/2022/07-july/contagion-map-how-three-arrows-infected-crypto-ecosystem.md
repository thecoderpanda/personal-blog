# The Contagion Map: How Three Arrows Infected the Entire Crypto Ecosystem

> **TL;DR:** The collapse of Three Arrows Capital (3AC) was not an isolated event, but the detonator for a systemic contagion that wiped out billions in centralized crypto lending. By mapping their massive web of unsecured loans from Voyager, BlockFi, and Genesis, we can trace exactly how a single hedge fund's reckless directional bets brought down the pillars of crypto shadow banking.

It turns out that if you borrow billions of dollars from everyone in town and bet it all on a algorithmic stablecoin that goes to zero, people eventually want their money back. And when you cannot pay them back because you bought a superyacht instead of hedging your downside, the entire neighborhood burns down with you. Welcome to the great crypto contagion of 2022, where the match was struck by a single Singaporean hedge fund called Three Arrows Capital, or 3AC.

For years, founders Zhu Su and Kyle Davies were treated like the smartest guys in the room. They preached the gospel of the "supercycle," a theoretical paradise where crypto would only go up, making traditional market cycles obsolete. They didn't just drink their own Kool-Aid; they hooked themselves up to an intravenous drip of it. When the Terra/Luna ecosystem collapsed in May, the supercycle thesis met reality at terminal velocity. Underneath the bravado lay a horrifyingly fragile house of cards built on recursive leverage, unhedged directional long bets, and a network of lenders who completely abandoned basic risk management in exchange for vanity yields.

## The Anatomy of the 3AC Black Hole

To understand how a single fund could infect the entire industry, you have to understand their business model: weaponized prestige. Three Arrows Capital used its massive balance sheet and early venture success to establish an aura of absolute invincibility. Lenders did not perform due diligence on 3AC because they were terrified of losing their business. If Genesis or Voyager demanded to see audited financial statements, Zhu and Kyle would simply threaten to take their multi-hundred-million-dollar borrowing business elsewhere. 

This leverage allowed them to borrow from dozens of different counterparties simultaneously using the exact same collateral—or in many cases, no collateral at all. They took these borrowed funds and plunged them into highly illiquid positions, such as Grayscale Bitcoin Trust (GBTC), staked Ethereum (stETH), and early-stage venture deals. When these assets began trading at steep discounts to their underlying values, 3AC was hit with margin calls they could not meet. Instead of de-risking, they ghosted their lenders, went radio silent, and let the liquidations begin.

## Mapping the Devastation: Who Held the Bags?

The list of institutions infected by 3AC reads like a directory of the top centralized finance (CeFi) players of the bull run. Let us trace the primary paths of the infection:

- **Voyager Digital**: Perhaps the most egregious example of failed risk management. Voyager loaned 3AC 15,250 BTC and 350 million USDC without demanding any collateral. When 3AC defaulted, Voyager was left with a massive hole in its balance sheet, forcing them to suspend trading, withdrawals, and deposits before filing for Chapter 11 bankruptcy.
- **Genesis Trading**: The institutional lending giant parent company, Digital Currency Group (DCG), had exposed Genesis to the tune of 2.36 billion dollars loaned to 3AC. Although Genesis liquidated the collateral they held, they were still left with a staggering 1.2 billion dollar claim, which DCG had to step in and assume to keep their subsidiary afloat.
- **BlockFi**: After 3AC failed to meet its margin calls, BlockFi liquidated their positions, resulting in a loss of approximately 80 million dollars. This left BlockFi severely undercapitalized, forcing them to seek a rescue credit facility from FTX, which ultimately sealed their fate as another domino destined to fall.
- **Blockchain.com**: This veteran wallet and exchange service suffered a 270 million dollar hit from its loan exposure to the fund, leading to significant layoffs and asset write-downs.

This was not a complex financial crisis born of exotic derivatives. It was a classic, run-of-the-mill debt spiral. 3AC borrowed money from Peter to pay Paul, used Paul's money to buy illiquid tokens, and when the market turned, they had nothing but bad vibes and empty promises to return.

## The Mirage of Centralized Yield

The collapse of 3AC exposed the fundamental flaw of centralized yield structures in crypto. These companies marketed themselves as high-yield savings accounts, safe havens for retail investors who wanted to earn 8% on their stablecoins without dealing with the complexities of decentralized finance. But beneath the polished user interfaces and friendly mobile apps, these platforms were operating as highly unregulated, highly leveraged shadow banks.

In traditional finance, banks are subject to strict reserve requirements, capital adequacy ratios, and transparent reporting. In the CeFi shadow banking system of 2022, risk management was replaced by "trust me, bro." Lenders took customer deposits and handed them to a single hedge fund with zero transparency. The irony is painful: crypto was built to eliminate trust in centralized intermediaries, yet millions of users handed their life savings to centralized middlemen who in turn handed it to two guys who spent their days tweeting philosophy and their nights losing money on leveraged long positions.

## The Hard Lesson of 2022

If there is a silver lining to this wreckage, it is the stark contrast between CeFi and DeFi. While Voyager, Celsius, and BlockFi were collapsing into bankruptcy courts, decentralized lending protocols like Aave, MakerDAO, and Compound functioned exactly as programmed. 

The smart contracts did not care about Zhu Su's Twitter following or Kyle Davies' prestige. When 3AC's positions on Aave fell below the liquidation threshold, the protocol's code automatically liquidated their collateral in real-time, block by block, without human intervention or backroom negotiations. DeFi did not need a bailout because DeFi does not rely on trust. As we pick up the pieces of this bear market, the path forward is clear: if you cannot verify it on-chain, it does not exist.

## Key Takeaways
- **Prestige is not collateral**: Lenders abandoned standard credit checks and due diligence because they were blinded by the perceived prestige and size of Three Arrows Capital.
- **CeFi was a shadow banking illusion**: Centralized yield platforms were not low-risk savings accounts; they were highly leveraged lending desks taking massive directional risks with retail deposits.
- **DeFi survived while CeFi died**: Smart-contract based lending protocols executed liquidations automatically and remained fully solvent, proving that code-based trustless systems are inherently more robust than opaque human-led institutions.
- **Systemic leverage is a silent killer**: The interconnectedness of crypto lending meant that a default at the top of the food chain instantly triggered a domino effect across the entire ecosystem.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Why didn't 3AC lenders demand more collateral?**
A: Lenders were engaged in a race to the bottom to capture institutional borrowing business. Because 3AC was considered the premier fund in the space, lenders feared that demanding strict collateral terms or audited financials would cause 3AC to take their business to a competitor.

**Q: How did the Terra/Luna collapse affect Three Arrows Capital?**
A: 3AC had invested over 200 million dollars of their own capital (and borrowed funds) into Terra's LUNA token. When LUNA fell to zero in May 2022, it wiped out a significant portion of 3AC's balance sheet, triggering a chain reaction of margin calls they could not meet.

**Q: What is the difference between how DeFi and CeFi handled the liquidation of 3AC?**
A: CeFi lenders handled liquidations through backroom phone calls, delays, and eventual bankruptcy filings due to undercollateralized loans. DeFi protocols liquidated 3AC's collateral automatically on-chain through smart contracts, ensuring the protocols remained fully solvent and collateralized.
