# Three Arrows Capital: The Crypto Hedge Fund That Collapsed the Market

> **TL;DR:** Three Arrows Capital (3AC) was the crown jewel of crypto hedge funds, but their massive bets on the "Supercycle" were funded by a precarious house of cards. When their leveraged positions in Terra/Luna and Grayscale's Bitcoin Trust imploded, they defaulted on billions in uncollateralized loans, dragging down Voyager, BlockFi, and Genesis in a brutal display of financial contagion.

If you spent any time on crypto Twitter over the past two years, you knew Su Zhu and Kyle Davies. They were the brilliant minds behind Three Arrows Capital (3AC), a Singapore-based hedge fund that managed billions of dollars. They were the undisputed royalty of the bull run. Su Zhu, in particular, was the high priest of the "Supercycle" thesis—the idea that crypto was entering a structural bull market that would never experience another 80% drawdown. Millions of retail investors hung on their every tweet, copy-trading their way into whatever altcoin 3AC was pumping.

Today, those same brilliant minds have apparently gone completely dark, leaving behind a multi-billion dollar crater in the middle of the crypto industry. The Supercycle is not just dead; it has been cremated, and its ashes have been scattered over the bankrupt carcasses of some of the largest institutional lending desks in the world. As it turns out, 3AC’s genius was not a superior understanding of market dynamics. Their genius was a masterclass in convincing everyone to lend them money without collateral, which they then gambled away on highly correlated, directional bull-market bets.

## The Cult of the Supercycle

To understand how we got here, we have to look back at the sheer hubris that dominated late 2021 and early 2022. The Supercycle was not just a market prediction; it was a religious dogma. The premise was simple: institutional adoption was so massive, and the demand for crypto so structural, that the old four-year halving cycle was broken. We were going up forever, with only minor bumps along the way.

Because Su Zhu and Kyle Davies believed their own marketing, they positioned 3AC with extreme, directional leverage. They did not hedge. They did not run a balanced book. They took customer capital, borrowed institutional capital, and poured it directly into long positions on some of the most volatile assets in the space. They were massive holders of Avalanche, Near, Polkadot, and, most catastrophically, Terra (LUNA). When you believe the market cannot drop, risk management feels like an unnecessary drag on your performance metrics.

## The Cascade of Borrowed Billions

The true horror of the 3AC collapse is not that a hedge fund made bad bets. Funds blow up all the time. The horror is how they managed to fund those bets. Because of their reputation, institutional lenders were desperate to do business with 3AC. Lenders like Genesis, Voyager Digital, BlockFi, and Celsius competed to give 3AC loans on incredibly generous terms. 

In many cases, these loans were virtually uncollateralized. 3AC would show up, demand $100 million in BTC or stablecoins, and lenders would hand it over with little more than a handshake and a promise. Why? Because 3AC was "too big to fail" and "too smart to lose." Lenders assumed 3AC was market-neutral or possessed secret, sophisticated hedging strategies. Instead, 3AC was taking those borrowed billions and double-downing on their favorite tokens, using the same assets as collateral across multiple different lenders to borrow even more.

## The Luna Implosion and the Margin Calls

The first domino fell in May 2022 when the Terra/Luna algorithmic stablecoin ecosystem collapsed. 3AC had invested over $200 million in locked LUNA, which went to virtually zero in a matter of days. But the damage was far deeper than just the loss of that initial capital. The collapse of LUNA triggered a massive drawdown across the entire crypto market, which in turn depressed the price of Ethereum and Bitcoin.

At the same time, 3AC was heavily exposed to the Grayscale Bitcoin Trust (GBTC) arbitrage trade. For years, GBTC traded at a premium to spot Bitcoin, allowing funds to buy BTC, convert it to GBTC, and sell it for an easy profit. But in late 2021, that premium flipped into a massive, widening discount. 3AC’s position in GBTC was deeply underwater, and they could not exit because the shares were locked. As asset prices plummeted, lenders began issuing margin calls. 3AC did not have the cash or liquid assets to meet them. Instead of answering the phone, they stopped responding entirely, leaving their counterparties to realize they had lent billions to an empty shell.

## The House of Cards Collapses

When 3AC failed to meet its margin calls, lenders were forced to liquidate 3AC's collateral. But because the loans were under-collateralized and the market was illiquid, these liquidations created massive bad debt on the balance sheets of the lenders themselves. Voyager Digital had lent 3AC over $650 million with zero collateral. BlockFi had lent them hundreds of millions. Genesis was exposed to the tune of $2.3 billion.

The contagion spread instantly. Voyager was forced to suspend withdrawals, leaving retail depositors locked out of their accounts. BlockFi had to accept a bailout from FTX just to survive. Genesis faced a massive capital shortfall. This was not a decentralized financial crisis; this was a classic, old-school credit bubble driven by greed, lack of transparency, and a complete absence of basic counterparty due diligence. It is the exact same behavior that caused the 2008 global financial crisis, dressed up in web3 terminology.

## Key Takeaways
- **Reputational Risk is Not Collateral**: Convincing lenders you are a genius is not the same as having collateral. Institutional crypto lending proved to be shockingly amateurish and prone to relationship-driven bubble building.
- **Unhedged Leverage is a Death Sentence**: Directional leverage in a volatile market works until it doesn't. When the trend reverses, unhedged long positions with borrowed capital collapse exponentially.
- **On-chain Transparency is Essential**: The institutional lending market occurred off-chain in private agreements. Had these positions been transparent and smart-contract-governed, liquidations would have happened automatically before bad debt could accumulate.
- **The Contagion is Never Isolated**: In a highly connected ecosystem, the failure of a single massive player can trigger a systemic liquidity freeze across dozens of supposedly independent platforms.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Where did Su Zhu and Kyle Davies actually go?**
A: Following the fund's insolvency, both founders went into hiding, citing safety concerns. They have since popped up occasionally on social media and in foreign jurisdictions, but their disappearance immediately after the collapse left liquidators struggling to secure remaining assets for creditors.

**Q: Why didn't institutional lenders demand collateral from 3AC?**
A: Greed and FOMO. Lenders were desperate to capture yield and generate loan volume. 3AC was so prestigious that if one lender demanded strict collateral terms, 3AC would simply go to a competitor who was willing to offer easier terms. This created a race to the bottom in risk standards.

**Q: How does this collapse affect retail investors who didn't use 3AC?**
A: Even if you never heard of 3AC, you might have held funds in Voyager, Celsius, or BlockFi. Because those platforms lent your deposits to 3AC, their insolvency directly led to the freezing of your accounts. It is a brutal reminder that centralized yield platforms are highly interconnected.
